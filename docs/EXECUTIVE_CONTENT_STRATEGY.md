# Executive content & visual strategy

## Decision

The site should behave less like a long-form manifesto and more like an executive decision surface.

The core audience is a CEO, CIO/CTO, transformation leader or enterprise-architecture leader who arrives with limited attention and one practical question:

> Is this person useful for the problem I have now, and what will I get if we work together?

The previous version was intellectually coherent but made the visitor assemble the commercial value proposition from many sections. It explained the worldview before making the offer legible. For a senior executive that creates unnecessary cognitive cost.

The new hierarchy is:

1. **business tension** — where value is lost;
2. **executive outcome** — what changes after the work;
3. **concrete outputs** — what the client actually receives;
4. **proof** — examples and credibility;
5. **method** — OAF as the integrating lens;
6. **deeper thinking** — Perspective / research / lineage.

OAF remains important, but it moves from “thing to understand first” to “reason the work is coherent”.

## What strong peers do

The review focused on a mix of top-tier consulting, modern transformation studios and individual management thinkers.

### Board of Innovation

Their strongest pattern is brutal clarity of outcome. The site opens with a simple position, then moves quickly into what the firm changes, quantified proof, concrete capabilities and case studies. Service pages explicitly answer “how we solve it” and “outcomes”. Visuals carry a large part of the explanation instead of forcing every concept into prose.

Reference: https://www.boardofinnovation.com/ and https://www.boardofinnovation.com/services/operating-model/

### NOBL

NOBL leads with the leadership problem rather than internal consulting vocabulary: organizations struggle to execute strategy, decisions are fuzzy, projects stall, complexity grows. Capabilities are framed as recognizable executive situations. Their methodology sits behind the offer, not in front of it.

Reference: https://nobl.io/ and https://go.nobl.io/help/what-exactly-does-nobl-do

### Kearney

Kearney connects organization and operating-model work directly to margin, performance, friction and resilience. The important lesson is the bridge from abstract organization design to business consequence.

Reference: https://www.kearney.com/service/organization

### McKinsey

The strongest current transformation content repeatedly connects operating model, governance and execution to business outcomes, and frames the CEO as the integrating owner of the system. The language is useful because it moves from transformation activity to operating rhythm, accountability, value drivers and P&L consequences.

References:
- https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/transformation/our-insights/collective-action-collective-success-a-ceos-role-in-transformations
- https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/people-and-organization/our-insights/a-new-operating-model-for-a-new-world

### Roger Martin / Rita McGrath

Individual thinker sites do one thing particularly well: role clarity and authority appear very early. “Thinker / Author / Advisor / Speaker” or “Author / Speaker / Strategist” is simple, legible and commercially useful. Expertise, recognition, work-with-me paths and current thinking are separated rather than mixed into one long narrative.

References:
- https://rogerlmartin.com/
- https://www.ritamcgrath.com/

### Combined lesson

The high-performing pattern is not “less sophisticated”. It is **more layered**:

- first screen = relevance;
- second screen = recognizable problems;
- third screen = what the buyer receives;
- then proof;
- methodology and intellectual lineage later.

That is the direction adopted here.

## Diagnosis of the previous site

### 1. Strong thesis, weak first-order commercial clarity — HIGH

The site explained that strategy, architecture, portfolio, governance and execution need coherence. That is credible, but a CEO/CIO still had to infer what Arkadiusz would actually do in a real engagement.

**Impact:** interest without a clear next step; risk of being perceived as a thought-leadership site rather than an advisory proposition.

**Fix:** state the audience, tension, outcome and engagement value in the hero; route the primary CTA to Advisory, not Perspective.

### 2. “Practice is not a services catalogue” worked against the buyer — HIGH

Intellectually this protected the brand from generic consulting language. Commercially it removed the answer to “what can I engage you for?”.

**Impact:** unnecessary ambiguity at the moment a senior buyer needs a concrete mental model.

**Fix:** retain the anti-template stance but introduce three engagement formats: Diagnostic, Design, Advisory.

### 3. Too much text before enough visual compression — HIGH

Many sections used a heading + paragraph + several articles containing another heading + paragraph. Even when the copy was good, the interaction pattern repeated too often.

**Impact:** high reading cost, low visual memory, weak scanability on mobile and desktop.

**Fix:** every major abstract concept now gets a visual carrier: decision-system map, trigger grid, output blueprint, case flow, OAF model.

### 4. Methodology appeared before enough buyer value — MEDIUM/HIGH

OAF was distinctive but required a visitor to invest cognitive effort before understanding the practical offer.

**Fix:** OAF remains a named asset, but the homepage describes it as the thinking layer behind the work. The dedicated OAF page uses progressive disclosure for lineage and principles.

### 5. Case studies were credible but too documentary — MEDIUM

The old format was careful and honest, but context/challenge/approach/value across six cases created a long ledger.

**Fix:** homepage uses only three cases and visually compresses them into **friction → shift → value**. Advisory keeps four deeper examples. No invented metrics are introduced.

### 6. Navigation reflected the author’s taxonomy more than the buyer’s intent — MEDIUM

“Praktyka” was less obvious than “Współpraca”. The order also sent visitors into Perspective/OAF before the practical offer.

**Fix:** primary navigation begins with **Współpraca / Advisory**, then OAF, Perspective, About and Contact. Contact receives a stronger visual treatment.

## New executive narrative

### Six-second answer

**What is this?**  
Executive advisory for problems that cross strategy, operating model, enterprise architecture, portfolio and execution.

**Why care?**  
Because value is often lost at the handoffs between those functions, not inside them.

**What changes?**  
Fewer competing priorities, clearer decision rights, better investment sequencing and a shorter path from direction to outcome.

### Thirty-second answer

Arkadiusz helps leadership teams diagnose where decision coherence breaks, design the target decision architecture, connect it to portfolio choices and governance, and create a transition roadmap with an evidence loop. The work is deliberately cross-functional because the problem usually sits between formal owners.

### Two-minute answer

The buyer can then inspect:

- trigger situations;
- concrete artifacts;
- engagement formats;
- anonymized examples;
- OAF as the integrating method;
- deeper research and perspective.

## Information architecture

### Home

Purpose: answer relevance and value quickly.

Sections:

1. Hero + decision-system visual
2. Four executive trigger situations
3. “What you actually get” blueprint
4. Three proof cases
5. OAF as method
6. Direct CTA

### Advisory / Współpraca

Purpose: make the commercial offer concrete without becoming a generic consulting menu.

Sections:

1. Executive advisory positioning
2. Three engagement formats
3. Output blueprint
4. Four connected domains
5. Four selected cases
6. Contact CTA

### OAF

Purpose: explain the method to an interested executive or practitioner without forcing everyone through the academic depth.

Visible by default:

1. four executive questions;
2. model visual;
3. misfit examples;
4. use cases.

Progressive disclosure:

- intellectual lineage;
- decision-quality principles.

### Perspective

Purpose: thought leadership, research and evidence. It should not carry the burden of explaining the service proposition.

### About

Purpose: answer “why this person?” through trajectory, cross-functional experience and operating principles.

### Contact

Purpose: collect a concrete context and decision problem, not a generic lead.

## Design system direction

### Visual narrative

The visual language is **executive systems engineering**, not “consulting cards” and not generic SaaS.

Core motifs:

- connected nodes;
- thin decision paths;
- handoff points;
- current-state / target-state blueprints;
- feedback loops;
- numbered evidence rails;
- controlled dark “system” surfaces against warm paper backgrounds.

### Graphics

#### Decision system map

Strategy → operating model → architecture → portfolio → execution, with an explicit evidence loop back to the next decision.

The visual carries the central thesis in seconds and creates a reusable brand device.

#### Trigger signals

Four large editorial quadrants with a small crosshair/signal mark. They are designed to be scanned as situations, not read as service cards.

#### Output blueprint

Five rows pair a mini architectural drawing with:

- the artifact;
- the output name;
- the executive question it answers.

This is the most important conversion improvement because it makes the work tangible.

#### Case flow

Each case is compressed into a visual logic:

**friction → intervention → management value**.

#### OAF

The circular model remains because it is a distinctive system visual, but it appears after value has been established.

### Motion

Motion is intentionally restrained:

- animated flow line in the decision system;
- subtle pulse at risk/handoff points;
- no scroll hijacking;
- no custom cursor;
- no heavy JS;
- all motion disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## Content rules going forward

1. One section = one executive question.
2. Headlines should make a claim, not name a topic.
3. Avoid two consecutive text-heavy sections.
4. If a concept can be shown as a flow, map, decision tree or before/after, prefer the visual.
5. Avoid unexplained acronyms above the fold.
6. OAF is a method, not the primary product promise.
7. Every commercial page must answer: **when to call / what happens / what I receive / why trust this / what next**.
8. No invented client names, logos, KPI or pseudo-precision.
9. Research belongs in Perspective or expandable supporting content, not in the critical conversion path.
10. The tone should sound like a senior operator talking to another senior operator: precise, low-drama, concrete.

| Issue | Severity | Business/user impact | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Offer unclear for CEO/CIO | High | Visitor cannot map expertise to a buying situation | Executive hero + Advisory page + engagement formats |
| Too much repeated prose | High | Scan fatigue and weak retention | Visual system map, trigger grid, blueprint and compressed cases |
| OAF cognitive load | High | Method competes with value proposition | Method moved later; OAF page simplified; lineage/principles collapsed |
| Weak “what do I get?” | High | Low conversion confidence | Five concrete output artifacts |
| Practice label ambiguous | Medium | Navigation friction | Rename navigation label to Współpraca / Advisory |
| Cases too long | Medium | Proof becomes reading assignment | 3 home cases, 4 advisory cases, friction → shift → value |
| Motion risk | Low | Potential accessibility/performance issue | CSS-only, restrained and reduced-motion aware |
