# Information Architecture — executive advisory platform

## Product thesis

The site is a personal executive-advisory platform with an intellectual product behind it. It must answer two different jobs without confusing them:

1. **buyer job:** “Is this useful for the problem I have now, what changes, and what will I actually receive?”
2. **thinker job:** “What is the underlying point of view, evidence base and method?”

The buyer job comes first on Home and Advisory. The deeper intellectual job is served by OAF and Perspective.

The primary narrative is therefore:

**recognizable executive tension → business outcome → tangible outputs → proof → method → deeper thinking → contact**

This is deliberately different from a framework-first or biography-first personal site. A CEO/CIO should not have to decode the author’s taxonomy before understanding the practical value.

## One information architecture, two translations

PL and EN use the same route keys and page structure. English differs by the `/en` prefix and translated content.

Canonical pairs:

- `/` ↔ `/en`
- `/about` ↔ `/en/about`
- `/oaf` ↔ `/en/oaf`
- `/work` ↔ `/en/work`
- `/writing` ↔ `/en/writing`
- `/contact` ↔ `/en/contact`
- `/privacy` ↔ `/en/privacy`
- `/writing/<slug>` ↔ `/en/writing/<slug>`

The language switch preserves the current page and, for essays, the current article slug.

## Primary navigation

1. **Współpraca / Advisory**
2. **OAF**
3. **Perspektywa / Perspective**
4. **O mnie / About**
5. **Kontakt / Contact**

The brand links to Home. Contact receives stronger visual emphasis.

This order follows buyer intent:

**what can we do together? → how do you think? → show me the depth → why you? → act**

## Home — executive relevance and value

Purpose: make the offer legible in seconds without flattening the intellectual character.

Sequence:

1. **Hero:** strategy-to-execution tension + executive outcome + primary CTA to Advisory.
2. **Decision-system visual:** Direction → Operating model → Architecture → Portfolio → Execution + evidence loop.
3. **Four trigger situations:** recognizable CEO/CIO symptoms rather than service categories.
4. **What you actually get:** five concrete artifacts / decision outputs.
5. **Proof:** three anonymized cases compressed to friction → shift → management value.
6. **Method:** OAF as the integrating thinking layer, not the first thing the visitor has to learn.
7. **Close:** one concrete invitation — describe a decision stuck between silos.

Home deliberately excludes:

- employment-title positioning;
- credentials above the fold;
- long research ledgers;
- a full OAF theory explanation;
- a six-case documentary archive;
- generic “services” cards.

Visual grammar: large editorial typography + a dark decision-system diagram + numbered quadrants + blueprint rows + case flows. Abstract claims should alternate with visual compression.

## Advisory / Współpraca — commercial clarity without a generic services catalogue

Purpose: answer “what does working together look like?” while preserving a senior, problem-led positioning.

Sequence:

1. Hero: one decision crossing strategy, organization, technology and portfolio.
2. Three engagement formats:
   - **Diagnostic** — locate the system problem and executive choices;
   - **Design** — redesign operating/decision architecture and transition;
   - **Advisory** — improve portfolio/transformation cadence and feedback.
3. Output blueprint: the same concrete decision artifacts seen on Home, now in fuller context.
4. Connected domains: Strategy & operating model, Enterprise architecture, Portfolio & PMO, AI & technology governance.
5. Four selected anonymized cases.
6. Contact CTA.

The page is not a fixed product menu. Engagement format describes **how to enter the problem**, not a pre-packaged scope.

Case-study rule: no invented client names, logos, revenue numbers or unsupported KPI. Public proof is structured as **problem → intervention → management value**.

## OAF — method / intellectual product

Purpose: explain the integrating logic to executives and practitioners who want to understand the method after the value proposition is clear.

Visible sequence:

1. Hero: OAF as four questions / one decision loop.
2. Four executive questions:
   - Direction
   - Architecture / operating capability
   - Priorities / portfolio
   - Evidence / execution
3. Circular OAF model.
4. Fit/misfit examples.
5. Application contexts.
6. CTA to Advisory / Contact.

Progressive disclosure:

- intellectual lineage;
- decision-quality principles;
- explicit boundaries/non-goals.

This keeps intellectual honesty and sourcing without making every senior visitor read the research history before understanding the model.

Visual grammar: one strong system diagram, four-question grid, misfit rail and expandable supporting depth. Mobile falls back to a semantic linear model.

## Perspective — evidence and thinking

Purpose: establish depth, currency and intellectual credibility. Perspective no longer carries the burden of explaining the commercial offer.

Sequence:

1. Hero.
2. Research lens / recurring executive questions.
3. Evidence ledger.
4. Featured essay.
5. Essay library.
6. Publishing standard: observation → evidence → implication.
7. Bridge to OAF.

Essay cards use category labels without artificial reading-time precision.

Visual grammar: research notes, ledger, one featured article and compact editorial library — not a generic blog-card wall.

## About — “why this person?”

Purpose: explain the source of the cross-system perspective without tying the brand to one employer or current title.

Sequence:

1. restrained professional portrait;
2. personal story: technology/delivery → EA → transformation → strategy/portfolio/PMO;
3. brand profile defined by the intersection of disciplines;
4. trajectory river;
5. working principles;
6. credentials lower in hierarchy;
7. next path to Perspective or Contact.

Employment titles must not become the primary brand statement.

## Contact — decision-context intake

Purpose: make first contact useful rather than generic.

Structure:

1. “Start with the problem” framing.
2. Short guidance: context, decision, constraints.
3. Accessible contact form.
4. LinkedIn fallback.
5. Privacy disclosure.

The form is not a newsletter/CRM capture. At launch it only supports direct contact.

## Executive scan paths

### Six seconds

The visitor should be able to answer:

- what is this?
- for whom?
- what problem class?
- why care?

### Thirty seconds

The visitor should identify:

- four recognizable trigger situations;
- the type of management outcome;
- what the client actually receives.

### Two minutes

The visitor should be able to inspect:

- engagement formats;
- proof/cases;
- OAF method;
- credibility and deeper thinking.

This replaces the old linear assumption that every visitor should move Home → Perspective → OAF → Practice → About → Contact.

## Linking rule

Each page exposes at most two strong next paths at a decision point:

- Home → Advisory / Contact; OAF later as method.
- Advisory → Contact / OAF.
- OAF → Advisory / Contact; Perspective remains a deeper evidence path.
- Perspective → OAF / relevant essay.
- About → Perspective / Contact.
- Contact → end state.

This keeps exploration possible without turning every section into a menu.

## Content density rule

1. Never place two long prose-heavy sections back-to-back on conversion pages.
2. One section should answer one executive question.
3. If a relationship can be shown as a flow/map/decision tree, show it rather than explaining it twice.
4. Detail that validates the method but is not needed for first-order understanding belongs behind progressive disclosure or on Perspective.
5. Home and Advisory prioritize consequences, choices and outputs over discipline vocabulary.
